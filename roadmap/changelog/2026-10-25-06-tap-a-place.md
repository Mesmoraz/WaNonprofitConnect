## 2026-10-25 · Tap a place on the map, like Seattle or Ballard, and see every organization there (S-144)

**Story S-144,** built to the brief in docs/ALPHA-BRIEFS.md ("Tap a place, see who's there"), approved Oct 25 (PR #60). It came from Taylor the same day: tap "Seattle" or "Ballard", see its boundary, and see what organizations are there.

### What changed

- **Town and neighborhood names on the map can be tapped.** Every Washington city and town, and Seattle's districts and neighborhoods (Ballard, Fremont, Capitol Hill). Other names, such as rivers or a Tacoma neighborhood, do nothing, as before.
- **Tapping one** moves the map to fit it, draws its outline in a thin blue-grey line, and fades the land outside a little.
- **The strip at the top** reads "213 organizations in Ballard · 1 joined", counting everyone inside: organizations that have joined and every Washington nonprofit on the IRS list. Tap it for the list: joined first, with their ring and "96 needs · 74 offers open", then everyone else under "Not here yet", A to Z, 50 at a time with "Show 50 more".
- **A "Ballard ×" chip** comes first in the chip row. Tap it to go back to "in view". Tapping another place name swaps to it. Pins and their cards work as before.
- **The kind-of-help chips** narrow the joined count and rows to organizations with that kind of help open.
- **Outlines come from free public data:** U.S. Census place boundaries (public domain) and the City of Seattle's Neighborhood Map Atlas (public domain dedication). `node scripts/fetch-places.js` regenerates the file, which loads on the first tap.
- **Two new public API routes:** `GET /api/v1/places` (the outlines) and `GET /api/v1/places/{id}/orgs` (everyone inside one).

Needed to make it true, beyond the brief: the demo puts its own organizations first as the joined ones, over the real list. Two new color tokens, `--map-place-line` and `--map-place-fade`.
