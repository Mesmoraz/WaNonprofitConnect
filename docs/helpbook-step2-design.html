Design brief · Step 2 · draft for discussion

HelpBook

Washington's 501(c)(3)s already exist in public data — 35,361 of them. HelpBook lets them claim their place, say what they need and what they have, and get connected to the right group or business. This brief lays out who uses it, the "watch this" story, low-fi wireframes, the data model, and the plan — before we commit to any UX.

Built on the working prototype: IRS data for all 42,510 WA exempt orgs is loaded, and the needs ↔ offers exchange (matching, accept/decline, messaging, live feed) runs end-to-end through the API and a CLI demo. Nothing in this brief is implemented yet.

The "watch this" story

One need, two nonprofits, six beats. Every screen in the wireframes maps to one of these.

Casey's intake laptops dieBallard Food Bank (EIN 91-1428805) can't register clients. Casey opens HelpBook and creates an account — email or Google, one screen.
Casey claims the orgTypes "Ballard Food" → the IRS record is already there. Verifies with a @ballardfoodbank.org email. Done in under a minute; no data entry.
Casey posts a Need"12 laptops for client intake stations" — Technology, Critical, needed by the 30th. Casey can also browse: every WA 501(c)(3), filterable by cause, city, size, tags.
Jordan gets alertedComputers 4 People in Need (Bellingham) has "we often have: laptops" on their profile. HelpBook scores the match and notifies Jordan: in-app, email, push.

They talk, then hand offA private thread: shipping address, timing, contact. Either side marks it complete. Both listings close as Fulfilled — and the story is on record for the next time.

What the prototype already proves (beats 3–6 run today via API + CLI): posting, scored match suggestions with plain-English reasons, propose → accept → message → complete, and a real-time feed where private threads stay private. What Step 2 adds: beats 1–2 (accounts and claiming), the alert that makes beat 4 happen without anyone searching, org profiles with tags, business and supporter accounts, and the web UI.

Data model

What exists now is the exchange cluster (IRS data, listings, matches, messages). Step 2 adds people, memberships, verification, tags, alerts, money, and moderation. Nothing about the exchange itself changes — the acting-org header simply becomes a real login. Relationships first, then what each table holds.

Verification

The question is always "is this person allowed to speak for this organization?" — so verification hangs off an OrgClaim with a pluggable method. We can ship one method and add others without touching the rest of the system.

Businesses: WA UBI lookup (state business license) + email domain + a payment method on file. Supporters: email + the join gate (PWYW payment or a donation receipt reviewed by admin). All three feed the same admin queue (W10).

Placements businesses can buy (allow-list — nothing else)

  • Sponsored offer — their own offer pinned at the top of its category feed.
  • Need sponsorship — "Fulfilled thanks to ⟨Business⟩" on a completed match they filled; shown on both orgs' profiles.
  • Category sponsor — "Technology needs — supported by ⟨Business⟩" banner on one category, one sponsor at a time.
  • Directory card — a business card in the "Businesses who give" directory.

Rules: every placement is reviewed by an admin before going live, labeled "Sponsored", tied to giving (no generic advertising, no political or fundraising-solicitation content), and never inserted into private threads.

Compliance line we don't cross: HelpBook charges for its own service. It never collects, holds, or routes donations to nonprofits — cash giving links out to the org's own page. Doing otherwise in Washington can make the platform a commercial fundraiser under RCW 19.09 (registration, contracts, reporting) and raises money-transmitter questions. The supporter "donation proof" gate is evidence of a gift made elsewhere, not a payment through us.

Matching & alerts

The prototype scores a Need against Offers using category, shared keywords, location, urgency, and quantity — and returns the reasons. Step 2 widens the inputs so groups are "determined to be related" even when there's no listing yet:

  • Org capability tags — "we often have: laptops" on Computers 4 People means Ballard's laptop need alerts them with no offer posted.
  • Cause relatedness — NTEE major groups that tend to trade (Food ↔ Human Services, Housing ↔ Materials) get a small boost; learned from completed matches over time.
  • Geography — county and radius instead of city string equality (ZIP centroids first, real geocoding later via the NCCS geocoded BMF).
  • History — orgs that completed a match before are more likely to again.

Alerts are rules, not spam: each org gets a default AlertRule generated from its tags, category, and area, editable from W7's "tune my alerts". Channels: in-app always; email and push opt-in. A digest option batches low-urgency matches daily; Critical needs go out immediately.

Decisions to make before building

  1. Can an unverified nonprofit user post? Proposed: browse and draft only until verified. Alternative: post with an "unverified" badge and hold messages. Trade-off is speed of first value vs. impersonation risk.
  2. Pay-what-you-want floor. $0 allowed (true PWYW, max signups) or a $1 floor (keeps a card on file, deters bots)? Proposed: $0 allowed, card optional.
  3. Can supporters respond to needs? Proposed: not in v1 — they follow and get alerts. v2 adds "I can help" personal offers once moderation exists.
  4. Business pricing and tiers. Two tiers by geography is the proposal; needs a number and a free trial decision.
  5. Placement allow-list. Confirm the four types above are the right four, and whether "category sponsor" is exclusive.
  6. "Let's collaborate" threads without a listing. Proposed: yes — a lightweight org-to-org thread type, rate-limited, since that's half of the ask ("we should collaborate on that").
  7. Geography unit. County + radius from ZIP centroid. Cheap and matches how people think about "near me".
  8. The name. "HelpBook" is the working title — worth a quick trademark/domain check before it appears on a screen.
  9. Web framework for the wireframes: Blazor (stays C#, shares DTOs) vs. React (bigger hiring pool, easier mobile-web parity). Doesn't need deciding until the wireframes are signed off.

Step 2 plan

Each phase is shippable on its own and demonstrable through the existing API and CLI before the UI exists. Order is chosen so the "watch this" story becomes real end-to-end as early as possible.
